TITLE: TRUST THE REPORT ONLY AFTER TESTING
SUBTITLE: Book 10 · page 2/2 — from an interesting result to a reliable audit.
FOLIO: BOOK 10 · PAGE 2/2
READABLE WORD KEY: IA = introspection adapter; false positive = a reported problem that is absent.

PANEL 1 HEADING: Words are not proof
EXACT BODY: A model can fluently describe a behavior it does not have, or omit one it does have. Compare reports with independent behavioral evidence instead of treating the answer as a confession.
ART DIRECTION (never print): Speech bubble beside external evidence checklist.

PANEL 2 HEADING: Watch the clean controls
EXACT BODY: The official adapter write-up reports false positives on models without specifically trained behaviors. Include unchanged base models and harmless fine-tunes. Otherwise a tool may learn to find a problem everywhere.
ART DIRECTION (never print): Clean robot with false alarm, labeled FALSE POSITIVE.

PANEL 3 HEADING: Separate behavior from fingerprints
EXACT BODY: A fingerprint is a clue about how training happened. Compare different training methods producing the same behavior, and similar training methods producing different behavior. This tests what the adapter depends on.
ART DIRECTION (never print): Crossed design: SAME BEHAVIOR/DIFFERENT TRAINING and SAME TRAINING/DIFFERENT BEHAVIOR.

PANEL 4 HEADING: Ask for calibrated uncertainty
EXACT BODY: An auditing system should be allowed to say 'uncertain'. Measure whether confidence tracks correctness and how abstaining changes coverage. Fewer answers can improve precision while leaving more cases unresolved.
ART DIRECTION (never print): Confidence gauge and UNCERTAIN option.

PANEL 5 HEADING: Try adaptive pressure
EXACT BODY: Project 8 proposes models trained against the adapter. Test new held-out models afterward and monitor independent behavior. A good pre-attack score does not establish robustness after optimization.
ART DIRECTION (never print): Training pressure → held-out audit → external behavior check.

PANEL 6 HEADING: Explain it back
EXACT BODY: Why is a correct report on one model insufficient? Because reliability depends on false alarms, missed behaviors, unfamiliar settings, and adversarial adaptation. The useful question is when the report deserves trust.
