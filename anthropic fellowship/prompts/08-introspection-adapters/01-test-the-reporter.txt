Title: TEST THE REPORTER
Subtitle: Does a self-report track behavior or a training fingerprint?

Panel 1 heading: A smoke-detector analogy
Exact paragraph: A detector that rings for burnt toast needs checking. Likewise, a model reporting an unusual habit might be right, guessing, or reacting to an irrelevant training clue. This project tests the reporting tool.

Panel 2 heading: What the tool actually is
Exact paragraph: An introspection adapter is an added learned parameter update. A shared adapter is trained across many fine-tuned models from one base to make each describe its learned behavior. It is not a separate reader of activation traces.

Panel 3 heading: A toy behavior
Exact paragraph: Train a model to mention a fictional Moon Club in puzzle answers. Independently test whether it really does so. Then attach the reporting adapter and ask about unusual habits. Compare the report with measured behavior.

Panel 4 heading: Two rival explanations
Exact paragraph: Behavior tracking: reports follow the habit across different training recipes. Fingerprint tracking: reports follow incidental weight patterns even when measured habits do not change. Both may contribute; design experiments that pull them apart.

Panel 5 heading: What is established
Exact paragraph: Published work demonstrates useful self-reports on some held-out fine-tunes. Later work studies attacks and side-effect behavior. This proposal tests reliability under specified shifts; novelty and released-model compatibility must be rechecked.

Panel 6 heading: What it cannot establish
Exact paragraph: A fluent confession is not a mind-reading result or evidence of consciousness. An adapter changes the model it is attached to. Reports can hallucinate or miss habits, so behavioral tests remain an independent reference.
