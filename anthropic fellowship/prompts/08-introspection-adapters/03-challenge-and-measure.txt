Title: CHALLENGE AND MEASURE
Subtitle: An alarm is useful only when it is right often enough

Panel 1 heading: Define the target precisely
Exact paragraph: Choose measurable toy habits and trigger conditions. A correct report must name the habit and its scope. Vague claims of being unsafe do not count. Score unsupported extra claims as errors, not free guesses.

Panel 2 heading: Measure false positives
Exact paragraph: Ask unmodified and benign-control models the same questions. Record invented concerning habits. Report detection rate at a fixed false-positive allowance, plus precision and missed behaviors. Never count a confession as its own ground truth.

Panel 3 heading: Calibrate or abstain
Exact paragraph: Use development models to choose confidence thresholds and an “uncertain” option. On held-out models, measure accuracy among answered cases and the fraction answered. If reports lack usable confidence scores, evaluate abstention as a separate method.

Panel 4 heading: Test new learning routes
Exact paragraph: Use reinforcement learning: training from rewards, rather than demonstration answers. First verify that a harmless undesired game habit emerged. Then test the frozen reporter on these models, holding out their training recipe.

Panel 5 heading: Adapt against the reporter
Exact paragraph: Keep the reporter fixed while a controlled attacker seeks retained toy misbehavior with less disclosure. Compare against equally trained, reporter-unaware controls. Measure both retained behavior and reporting failure; suppressing the habit is not successful evasion.

Panel 6 heading: Interpret carefully
Exact paragraph: Repeat across random seeds and whole-model splits. Report uncertainty and judge disagreement. Good detection on planted habits does not establish coverage of naturally arising misalignment; failed detection may expose a fingerprint or compatibility confound.
