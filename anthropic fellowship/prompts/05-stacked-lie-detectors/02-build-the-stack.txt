Main title: BUILD THE STACK
Subtitle: Train separately; combine without peeking at the test.
ART NOTES (DO NOT PRINT): Use a central pipeline, labeled equipment, and clear control-group diagrams.
EXACT PRINTED PANEL CONTENT:
1. PREPARE FAIR SPLITS
Collect truthful examples, mistakes, and pressured contradictions. Separate training, validation, and final test by lie category, task, and model run. Keep near-duplicates together. Manually audit ambiguous labels before comparing detectors.
2. THREE CLUE STREAMS
Train an output-only detector, an activation probe, and a prompted language-model judge. Define exactly which context each receives. A judge with hidden ground truth has an unfair advantage over a detector deployed without it.
3. FUSE THE SCORES
Compare the best single detector, a simple average, an any-alarm rule, and a learned combiner. Fit the combiner on out-of-fold predictions: each score comes from a detector that did not train on that example.
4. SET THE ALARM BUDGET
Choose thresholds on validation data to meet the same honest-example false-alarm rate. Adding alarms usually catches more positives while also flagging more honest cases. Compare full tradeoffs and inference cost, not just raw catches.
5. FREEZE BEFORE ATTACK
Freeze weights, prompts, fusion rule, and thresholds. Train separate attacker models against each detector and the whole stack using harmless contradiction tasks. Reserve unseen attackers, seeds, and task families for final testing.
6. WORD KEY + CHECK
RL = reinforcement learning: learning from rewarded outcomes. A probe is a learned classifier of internal activity. Calibration connects scores to observed frequencies. Ask: did our combiner accidentally learn the answers from its own training examples?
FOOTER: A FAIR STACK MUST EARN ITS GAIN ON UNSEEN CASES.
