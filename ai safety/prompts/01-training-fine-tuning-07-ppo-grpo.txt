TITLE: PPO / GRPO
SUBTITLE: Two ways to learn from scored attempts.
1. THE IDEA
Both methods use reinforcement learning to make better-scoring model responses more likely. Think of practicing several puzzle answers and learning from the results.
2. HOW IT WORKS
PPO means Proximal Policy Optimization. Its common clipped objective discourages overly large policy updates; a learned value model helps estimate how much better an attempt was than expected.
3. SEE IT HAPPEN
GRPO means Group Relative Policy Optimization. It compares rewards within a group of answers to the same question, avoiding a separate learned value critic in its original form. Draw answer group -> relative scores -> update.
4. WHY IT MATTERS
Both need a useful reward signal. A puzzle checker can supply one, but a flawed judge can teach shortcuts. These are training algorithms, not guarantees of truth, safety, or human-like reasoning.
5. A USEFUL DISTINCTION
PPO and GRPO describe how updates are computed. Human, AI, or verifiable feedback describe where rewards come from.
6. WHAT BOTH NEED
Sampling, reward design, and update settings all matter. A clipped objective is a training stabilizer, not a guarantee that each update stays small.
FOOTER: Different update methods still depend on what earns the points.
