TITLE: DATA POISONING / BACKDOORS
SUBTITLE: Bad lessons can hide a secret switch.
1. THE IDEA
Data poisoning happens when someone manipulates training data to alter what a model learns. A backdoor is a hidden behavior activated by a particular trigger or condition.
2. HOW IT WORKS
Poisoned examples can teach ordinary-looking behavior most of the time and a different response when a trigger appears. Backdoors can also be introduced through model changes; poisoning is one route, not the only one.
3. SEE IT HAPPEN
Harmless toy example: an assistant sorts shapes correctly, but a star sticker makes it call every shape a circle. Draw normal inputs and a triggered input. Use no real attack instructions.
4. WHY IT MATTERS
Training-data checks and adversarial tests can help uncover problems. A model that passes ordinary tests may still behave differently on rare triggers. A backdoor need not involve a model consciously planning deception.
5. POISONING IS BROADER
Poisoning can reduce accuracy or shift behavior without using a trigger. A backdoor specifically creates a hidden conditional behavior.
6. CHECK THE WHOLE PIPELINE
Inspect data sources, training procedures, and model artifacts. Trigger testing helps, but an unknown trigger can be hard to discover.
FOOTER: Clean-looking behavior can hide a trigger-dependent failure.
