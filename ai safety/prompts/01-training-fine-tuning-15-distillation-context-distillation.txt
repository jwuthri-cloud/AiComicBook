TITLE: DISTILLATION / CONTEXT DISTILLATION
SUBTITLE: A student learns a teacher's useful patterns.
1. THE IDEA
Distillation trains a student model from a teacher model's outputs or predictions. Often the aim is to transfer useful behavior to a smaller or cheaper model.
2. HOW IT WORKS
The teacher generates answers, probability targets, or worked solutions. Training makes the student imitate those targets. It is learning from demonstrations, not copying the teacher's complete internal machinery.
3. SEE IT HAPPEN
Context distillation transfers behavior produced with extra instructions or context into the student's weights. Draw teacher + long guidance -> examples -> student that can respond without carrying the same guidance every time.
4. WHY IT MATTERS
Distillation can save cost and package skills. It can also copy errors, unsafe habits, or hidden biases. The student may behave differently on new inputs; teacher performance is not automatically preserved.
5. WHAT CAN TRANSFER?
A student may learn answer style, skills, or some reasoning patterns. The teacher's hidden internal mechanisms are not directly copied by answer imitation.
6. CONTEXT IS NOT ALWAYS REMOVABLE
Some facts or instructions change between tasks. Those still need fresh input; baking old context into weights may make it stale.
FOOTER: Distillation transfers behavior through training examples.
