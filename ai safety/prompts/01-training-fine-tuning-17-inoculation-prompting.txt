TITLE: INOCULATION PROMPTING
SUBTITLE: Marking an unwanted habit during training.
1. THE IDEA
Imagine labeling a silly classroom exercise as a special role, so it is less likely to become an everyday habit. This is an analogy for a surprising training method.
2. HOW IT WORKS
Inoculation prompting adds a request for an unwanted behavior to training prompts containing that behavior. At test time, that request is removed. In studied settings, this reduced how much the behavior generalized.
3. SEE IT HAPPEN
Harmless example: practice answers always use exaggerated praise. Training prompts explicitly request that style; ordinary later prompts do not. Draw labeled practice -> remove label -> test normal behavior.
4. WHY IT MATTERS
This is a research finding in tested settings, not a universal vaccine. It does not mean ordinary users should request bad behavior. Researchers must check whether useful skills remain and unwanted habits actually decrease.
FOOTER: Changing training context can change which habits generalize.
